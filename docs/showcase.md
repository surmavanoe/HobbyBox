# Hobbybox showcase

## One-sentence introduction

Hobbybox brings films, series, anime, books and manga into one diary, helping people track their interests and find their next story through AI search and friend context.

## A 60–90 second demo

1. **Home:** explain the problem of scattered watchlists and reading lists, then switch between formats.
2. **Search:** filter by a category, genre and year range; open a title.
3. **AI search:** try “A dark manga about a notebook that kills people.” Explain that matches come from the connected catalog.
4. **Diary:** save a rating or review and add a title to the watchlist.
5. **Profile:** show the saved entry, five favorites and achievement progress.
6. **Friends:** show the sample feed. Describe live friend-aware AI as a separate backend feature that requires a signed-in account with friend data.

If a service is unavailable, use ordinary catalog browsing and explain the dependency. Do not present static sample activity as real users.

## LinkedIn project description

**Hobbybox — a unified entertainment diary and discovery app**

A working MVP that brings films, TV, anime, books and manga into one place. Users can log reviews, maintain a watchlist, showcase favorites and search for titles using plot descriptions. Built with React Native, Expo, TypeScript and Supabase, with server-side semantic search and support for authenticated friend-context filters.

Current scope: live catalog and AI search, device-local tracking, and a sample social feed. Cloud synchronization and live friend management are next.

## Suggested post

We built Hobbybox to solve a familiar problem: our watchlists, reading lists and friends' recommendations are scattered across different apps and chats.

Our MVP brings films, series, anime, books and manga into one diary. You can log what you finish, save what comes next, display your favorites and describe a half-remembered story to search for its title.

The stack is React Native, Expo, TypeScript and Supabase, with Lovable helping us develop the interface. The backend also supports recommendations filtered by authenticated friend ratings and library history.

It is an early prototype: tracking is currently device-local and the social feed uses sample activity. Connecting those flows to live accounts is the next step.

Which would be more useful to you: finding a forgotten title, or discovering something your friends already loved?
